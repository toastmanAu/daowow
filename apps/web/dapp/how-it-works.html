How it works

Prizes are funded by Nervos DAO yield on the pooled principal. Entrants get their principal back whether they win or lose. Nobody's stake pays anybody else's prize.

That is the whole design, and it is the opposite of a conventional lottery. There is no pot of losers' money. What is at stake is the interest the pool earns while it is locked — not the pool itself.

Where the prizes come from

Every ticket's principal is deposited into the Nervos DAO, the protocol-level savings mechanism built into CKB. A deposit earns yield over 180-epoch cycles, roughly a month each. A round runs for one or two of those cycles.

When the round settles, the principal returns to whoever deposited it, and the yield becomes the prize pool. A winner receives a share of that interest. A non-winner receives exactly what they put in.

This also means a round with few entrants has a small pool. Yield is proportional to how much was deposited and for how long — it is not topped up from anywhere.

How many places pay

More entrants, more places paid — the way a poker tournament pays more of a larger field. Each round commits to a payout table in advance, and a place only starts paying once the round reaches its threshold.

The table is fixed when the round is created and cannot be changed afterwards, by anyone, including whoever created it. The entry page shows the live table: how many places the round pays now, and what would open next.

Paying fewer, larger prizes in a small round is deliberate. A prize below about 61 CKB cannot even be paid into its own cell without being topped up, so spreading a small pool across five places would produce prizes too small to deliver.

How winners are chosen

First the entry list is sealed: once the round closes, the exact set of tickets is fixed and committed on chain. Only then is the randomness drawn.

The randomness comes from the hashes of four separate blocks, mined 64, 128, 192 and 256 blocks after the seal was recorded. Nobody — including whoever created the round — can know those hashes when entry closes, because the blocks do not exist yet. Those four hashes are combined with the round's own identity into a single seed, and winning ticket positions are drawn from it.

Anyone can recompute the result. The seal, the entropy record and the seed derivation are all public, so the draw can be checked independently rather than trusted.

What this is not

This is not a verifiable random function and not a randomness beacon. It relies on block hashes that miners produce. A miner who controlled the specific blocks used could in principle discard unfavourable ones and try again — "grinding" the result.

Sealing the entry list first means nobody can choose entropy to suit a field they already know, and using four blocks spread across roughly forty minutes makes influence far harder than a single block would. But the honest description is that this raises the cost of manipulation rather than making it impossible.

What a ticket actually is

Each ticket mints a claim NFT into your own wallet, in the same transaction as the entry. It is a real cell on chain, not a database row, and it carries the ticket's identity: which round, which position.

The claim holds the right to that ticket's prize. Your principal and any refund stay bound to you as the depositor and do not travel with it.

What it costs beyond the principal

On CKB, storing anything on chain means locking capacity for as long as it exists. A claim NFT needs about 222 CKB of capacity, and one is minted per ticket. An entry also needs about 237 CKB for its own record.

This capacity is locked, not spent. It returns to you when the claim is retired at the end of the round. The entry page quotes every component separately, so the total you confirm is the total committed.

How long your funds are committed

A round lasts one or two native DAO cycles — roughly 30 to 60 days. Your principal and the capacity above are locked for that whole period. Nervos DAO deposits cannot be withdrawn early without forfeiting the cycle's yield, which is why the duration is fixed when the round is created.

What works today

This is a testnet demonstration. Testnet CKB has no monetary value.

Working in this build:

  • Connecting a wallet and reading the live round from chain
  • Entering a round, which mints your claim NFTs
  • Refunding an entry the round did not accept

Specified and built in the contracts, but not yet available in this interface:

  • Transferring a claim to someone else
  • Redeeming a prize
  • Retiring a claim and receiving the winner's memento
  • The animated draw reveal

Those arrive in later milestones. Nothing on this page describes a feature as usable here before it is.
