How the savings pool works

Everyone's ticket money is pooled and placed in the Nervos DAO, a savings feature built into the Nervos network. While it sits there it earns interest.

When the round ends, every player gets their whole deposit back, win or lose. The interest the pool earned becomes the prizes.

How long it takes

You buy tickets while entries are open. Winners are drawn soon after entries close. Your deposit then stays in savings for the term, and comes back with any prize at payout.

What it costs

Nothing is spent except a small network fee. Everything else is a deposit that is returned to you.

What you can win

The more tickets a round sells, the more prize places it pays. The prize table is fixed when the round is created and nobody can change it afterwards.

My tickets

How it works

Prizes are funded by Nervos DAO yield on the pooled principal. Entrants get their principal back whether they win or lose. Nobody's stake pays anybody else's prize.

That is the whole design, and it is the opposite of a conventional lottery. There is no pot of losers' money. What is at stake is the interest the pool earns while it is locked, not the pool itself.

This page is the full detail, in the network's own terms. The other tabs say the same things in plainer words.

Where the prizes come from

Every ticket's principal is deposited into the Nervos DAO, the protocol-level savings mechanism built into CKB. A deposit earns yield over 180-epoch cycles, roughly a month each. A round runs for one or two of those cycles.

When the round settles, the principal returns to whoever deposited it, and the yield becomes the prize pool. A winner receives a share of that interest. A non-winner receives exactly what they put in.

This also means a round with few entrants has a small pool. Yield is proportional to how much was deposited and for how long. It is not topped up from anywhere.

How many places pay

More entrants, more places paid, the way a poker tournament pays more of a larger field. Each round commits to a payout table in advance, and a place only starts paying once the round reaches its threshold.

The table is fixed when the round is created and cannot be changed afterwards by anyone, including whoever created it. Home and the entry screen show the live table: how many places the round pays now, and what would open next.

Paying fewer, larger prizes in a small round is deliberate. A prize below about 61 CKB cannot even be paid into its own cell without being topped up, so spreading a small pool across five places would produce prizes too small to deliver.

How winners are chosen

First the entry list is sealed: once the round closes, the exact set of tickets is fixed and committed on chain. Only then is the randomness drawn.

The randomness comes from the hashes of four separate blocks: the block that sealed the entries, and the blocks 64, 128 and 192 after it. Nobody, including whoever created the round, can know the later hashes when entry closes, because those blocks do not exist yet. The four hashes are combined with the round's own identity into a single seed, and winning ticket positions are drawn from it.

The result is fixed the moment the last of those blocks is mined. This app works it out itself at that point and starts the draw. The round's operator writes the same result on chain once the seal is 256 blocks old, and the app checks that the two agree before showing anything.

Anyone can recompute the result. The seal, the block hashes and the seed derivation are all public, so the draw can be checked independently rather than trusted. The animated draw only reveals that result; it never chooses one.

What this is not

This is not a verifiable random function and not a randomness beacon. It relies on block hashes that miners produce. A miner who controlled the specific blocks used could in principle discard unfavourable ones and try again, "grinding" the result.

Sealing the entry list first means nobody can choose entropy to suit a field they already know, and using four blocks spread across roughly half an hour makes influence far harder than a single block would. But the honest description is that this raises the cost of manipulation rather than making it impossible.

This app also reads the chain through one public node. It checks the operator's record against the block hashes, but both come from that node, so the result on screen is as trustworthy as the node is.

What a ticket actually is

Each ticket mints a claim NFT into your own wallet, in the same transaction as the entry. It is a real cell on chain, not a database row, and it carries the ticket's identity: which round, which position.

The claim holds the right to that ticket's prize, and it can be sent to another wallet. Whoever holds a winning claim collects its prize, so My tickets shows the claims your wallet holds now. Your principal and any refund stay bound to you as the depositor and do not travel with it.

What it costs beyond the principal

On CKB, storing anything on chain means locking capacity for as long as it exists. A claim NFT needs about 222 CKB of capacity with a JoyID wallet, and one is minted per ticket. An entry also needs about 237 CKB for its own record. The other tabs call these the ticket deposit and the storage deposit.

This capacity is locked, not spent. It returns to you when the claim is retired at the end of the round. The entry screen lists every part separately, so the total you confirm is the total committed. The only thing spent is the network fee.

How long your funds are committed

A round lasts one or two native DAO cycles, roughly 30 to 60 days. Your principal and the capacity above are locked for that whole period. Nervos DAO deposits cannot be withdrawn early without forfeiting the cycle's yield, which is why the duration is fixed when the round is created.

Taking part in a test round

You need a JoyID wallet and some testnet CKB. Connect wallet creates a JoyID wallet if you don't have one. Testnet CKB is free from the Nervos faucet at faucet.nervos.org: paste your wallet address there. The entry screen lists what each ticket costs before you confirm anything.

This app lets each wallet hold up to 5 tickets in a round. That limit is the app's own rule and is not enforced by the network, so it is a courtesy between players, not a guarantee.

What works today

This is a testnet demonstration. Testnet CKB has no monetary value.

Working in this build:

  • Connecting a wallet once and seeing every round
  • Entering a round, which mints your claim NFTs
  • My tickets: what your wallet holds in every round, and whether it won
  • Refunding an entry the round did not accept, but only once the round has been frozen and its ledger closed. A passed deadline alone does not release an entry.
  • The animated draw, live as the result is fixed and as a replay afterwards

Specified and built in the contracts, but not yet available in this interface:

  • Transferring a claim to someone else
  • Claiming a prize (redeeming a winning claim)
  • Retiring a claim, which returns its ticket deposit and gives a winner their memento

Those arrive in later milestones. Nothing in this app describes a feature as usable here before it is.
